# Chapter 4
## Results

The present in-vitro investigation evaluated the wear resistance and surface roughness of two highly filled injectable composite resins, Beautifil Flow Plus X F00 and G-ænial Universal Injectable, in comparison with a conventional nanohybrid composite resin, Beautifil II LS, following a standardised toothbrushing simulation. Thirty-six bar-shaped specimens (n = 12 per group) were subjected to 10 000 brushing cycles under a constant load of 2 N in the presence of a toothpaste slurry prepared according to ISO recommendations. Wear was quantified by percentage weight loss, while surface topography was assessed by contact profilometry using the arithmetic mean roughness parameter (Ra).

### 4.1 Weight Loss after Simulated Toothbrushing

The mean specimen weights recorded before and after the abrasion protocol, together with the calculated percentage weight loss, are summarised in Table 4.1.

**Table 4.1** Mean weight (g) before and after simulated toothbrushing and percentage weight loss of the three experimental groups (n = 12)

| Group | Weight before (g) Mean ± SD | Weight after (g) Mean ± SD | Weight loss (%) Mean ± SD | p-value |
|-------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Beautifil Flow Plus X F00 | 0.1602 ± 0.0074 | 0.1576 ± 0.0031 | 1.49 ± 3.14 | |
| G-ænial Universal Injectable | 0.1630 ± 0.0102 | 0.1619 ± 0.0106 | 0.65 ± 0.37 | 0.329 |
| Beautifil II LS (Conventional) | 0.2313 ± 0.0154 | 0.2303 ± 0.0156 | 0.41 ± 0.25 | |

One-way analysis of variance indicated that the differences in percentage weight loss among the three materials did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.329). Numerically, Beautifil II LS exhibited the lowest mean weight loss, followed by G-ænial Universal Injectable, while Beautifil Flow Plus X F00 showed the highest mean value and the greatest inter-specimen variability. The elevated standard deviation observed in the Beautifil Flow Plus X group suggests heterogeneity in the response of individual specimens to the abrasive challenge.

### 4.2 Surface Roughness before and after Toothbrushing

Mean Ra values obtained before and after the 10 000 brushing cycles are presented in Table 4.2.

**Table 4.2** Mean surface roughness (Ra, µm) before and after simulated toothbrushing and the absolute change in Ra of the three groups (n = 12)

| Group | Ra before (µm) Mean ± SD | Ra after (µm) Mean ± SD | ΔRa (µm) Mean ± SD | p-value |
|-------|--------------------------|-------------------------|--------------------|--------|
| Beautifil Flow Plus X F00 | 0.038 ± 0.011 | 0.104 ± 0.017 | 0.066 ± 0.021 | |
| G-ænial Universal Injectable | 0.054 ± 0.010 | 0.100 ± 0.023 | 0.046 ± 0.023 | < 0.001 |
| Beautifil II LS (Conventional) | 0.119 ± 0.054 | 0.194 ± 0.050 | 0.075 ± 0.066 | |

At baseline the conventional nanohybrid composite already displayed higher mean roughness than the two injectable materials. After completion of the toothbrushing protocol, all groups demonstrated an increase in Ra. The final mean roughness values were 0.104 ± 0.017 µm for Beautifil Flow Plus X F00, 0.100 ± 0.023 µm for G-ænial Universal Injectable, and 0.194 ± 0.050 µm for Beautifil II LS. Kruskal-Wallis testing confirmed a statistically significant difference among the groups after abrasion (p < 0.001). Post-hoc comparisons indicated that the conventional nanohybrid resin remained significantly rougher than both injectable composites.

### 4.3 Overview of the Experimental Outcomes

Under the controlled three-body abrasion conditions employed in this study, the two highly filled injectable composites exhibited percentage weight loss that was statistically comparable to that of the conventional nanohybrid material. In contrast, both injectable resins maintained significantly lower surface roughness values after toothbrushing. These findings suggest that the injectable materials tested can provide wear behaviour similar to a conventional nanohybrid composite while offering a smoother surface after abrasive challenge.
